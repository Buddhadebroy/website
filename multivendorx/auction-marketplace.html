The dream

People pay more to win than to buy

Picture a marketplace where sellers list once, buyers compete and the price is set by how much people want the item. Timed listings, real-time bidding and a little exclusivity turn ordinary listings into events. Catawiki grew this way by ignoring "everything" and focusing on rare objects checked by experts, with weekly themed auctions.

Reality check

Is 90 days really possible? Yes, if you know what "done" means.

How it is possible

Discipline helps. A weekly system gets you there.

Motivation fades by week 3. A routine you decide once and repeat every week does not. This is the week that adds up to day 90.

Monday numbers · 1 hr

Bids per lot, sell-through rate and disputes. Then pick only three priorities.

Daily outreach · 30 min

Send 10 personal messages to sellers with items your bidders want.

Publish twice · 2 hrs

One preview of the next auction and one results recap with prices achieved.

Talk to 3 people · 1 hr

A seller, a winner and someone who was outbid. Each tells you something different.

Friday review · 30 min

On track? Keep going. Behind? Cut scope, not sleep, and push one goal to next week.

Lot 02 · Find

Pick Your Auction Niche (where bidders compete)

Auctions work best where items are scarce, valued differently by different people, and bought by people who care. Your task: write down 3 niches you already know and score each one on the three tests below. Keep the winner.

1Passion2Scarcity3Purchasing power Bids

Passion test

Do people collect it, join groups about it and talk about "the one that got away"? That's a niche bidders chase.

Scarcity test

Is each item one of a kind, limited or hard to price? Auctions find the price when nobody can agree on it.

Purchasing power test

Do buyers already spend hundreds on single items? Then a commission on the final price adds up.

Lot 03 · Find

The 72-Hour Validation Sprint

Spend three days proving bidders care before you spend three months building. Your task: run these three days back to back, then use the table to decide: build, narrow or switch.

Day 0-1

Listen in communities

Join 5-10 niche communities. Note complaints and the words people use, and rank pain points by how often and how strongly they come up.

Day 1-2

Teaser & waitlist

A one-page teaser for your first themed auction with a waitlist form. Share it where your niche hangs out.

Day 2-3

Interviews & outreach

Talk to 20 likely buyers about what they'd pay, and message 50 sellers about their hardest-to-price items.

After 72 hours, check your resultsA "rethink" costs you three days, not three months: narrow the niche (for example "watches" to "vintage chronographs") and run the sprint again.

Lot 12 · Grow

Advanced strategies, and what's next

Once weekly auctions run without you in every decision, these are the ideas worth testing. Your task: pick one, test it on one themed auction, and keep it only if bids go up.

Bundle auctions

Themed multi-seller bundles, with a small coordination fee on top of commission.

Fractional ownership

Let several buyers own a share of a high-value item. Check the regulations first.

AI assistance

Item identification, pricing hints, shill-bidding detection and smarter reserve prices.

Live & immersive

Creator-hosted live auctions, AR previews and provenance records for rare items.

The winning playbook: pick a niche you know deeply, solve trust better than anyone, scale what works and pivot fast, create urgency with countdowns and limited drops, and build a community around a shared passion.
