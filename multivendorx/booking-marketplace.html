The dream

Sell time, not stuff. Fill every slot.

Picture a marketplace where people browse, compare and book a service in a minute, and providers wake up to a full calendar. You don't deliver the service. You connect providers with customers and take a share of each booking. Headout grew this way: it solved "what can I do right now" for travellers, started in one city with hand-picked tour operators and chose quality over quantity.

Reality check

Is 90 days really possible? Yes, if you know what "done" means.

Find a Scheduling Problem (not just a category)

Strong booking niches fix a specific scheduling pain, not general convenience. Use the three Ps. Your task: write down 3 services you know and score each one on the three tests below. Keep the winner.

What providers struggle withCan't see open slots at a glanceUnclear confirmations and policiesPayment timing and no-showsPainful rescheduling
What customers hateCalling or messaging to check timesDouble bookingsPaying on the day with no protectionHearing nothing until the appointment

Passion test

Do you know this service from the inside, as a provider or a regular customer?

Pain test

Is booking it today a mess of calls, spreadsheets, double bookings and no-shows?

Payment potential test

Is the average booking $50+ and do customers book 2+ times a month?

The 72-Hour Validation Sprint

Spend three days proving providers and customers care before you spend three months building. Your task: run these three days back to back, then use the table to decide: build, narrow or switch.

Day 0-1

Map the market

Count providers in your city and category, and note how they take bookings today. Look for competitors with weak tools or unhappy users.

Day 1-2

Waitlist page

"Book [service] in [city] in one minute". Collect emails and ask how often they book.

Day 2-3

Provider outreach

Message 50 providers. Ask about empty slots, no-shows and how much time booking takes them.

After 72 hours, check your resultsA "rethink" costs you three days: narrow the service or the city and run the sprint again.

Revenue models for booking marketplaces

Most booking platforms take a share of each booking, then add provider plans and paid extras. Your task: start with a commission or a flat fee per booking. Add provider plans once providers see bookings.

ExtrasAdd-onsPaid placement, no-show cover, photography, promoted posts
Fine-tune later: vary commission by service type, give volume discounts and bonuses to top-rated providers, use seasonal rates and A/B test your rates against what customers and providers say.

Solving the cold start (providers first, always)

Customers leave an empty calendar and rarely come back. Recruit providers first, over eight weeks. Running a few bookings as a provider yourself teaches you their pain and earns early reviews. Your task: contact 50-100 providers personally and set up every calendar with them.

A booking flow people finish, and trust they feel

Every extra step loses customers. Design the journey minute by minute. Your task: book your own marketplace on a phone every week and time each step.

0-60 secSearch

Filters for location, service, availability, price and rating. Real-time availability.

1-3 minCompare

Detailed profiles, reviews, a 30-day calendar and upfront pricing with fees and deposits.

3-5 minBook

Three-step checkout, guest booking for first-timers, flexible payment, clear refunds.

BeforeRemind

Instant confirmation with a calendar invite, reminders at 24 h and 2 h, one-click rescheduling.

  • Verified providers: identity, licences, background checks
  • Reviews only from verified bookings
  • Secure payments and clear cancellation protection
  • Live chat and a fair dispute process

Scaling, and the 9 mistakes that sink booking platforms

Balance provider quality with coverage, spread demand across time slots and expand only when your first city runs smoothly. Your task: check this list before every expansion.

1Calendar sync failures
2A weak mobile experience
3Poor provider onboarding
4Weak payment security
5Inconsistent service quality
6An overcomplicated booking flow
7Not enough customer support

9Expanding to new cities too early
